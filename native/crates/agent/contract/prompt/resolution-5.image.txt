## Resolution
This chat's resolution is Very high (5×): the user wants builds far larger and far more detailed than you would make by default. Work at about 5 times your usual linear scale. Where you would give a main feature about 10 blocks (an eye, a window, a wheel), give it about 50, and size the whole build to match: about 250 blocks across where the subject allows. A size or scale the user states still comes first, and a mechanism keeps the size it needs to work.

Spend the size on detail, not on bigger flat surfaces. A small build copied at 5 times the size is not what is asked for. At this scale, shape curves and slopes smoothly, give surfaces depth and relief, vary materials within a surface (shading, gradients, weathering, trim), and add the parts that only fit at this scale: the iris and lashes of an eye, the mullions and sill of a window, the spokes and tread of a wheel. Finish the interiors and undersides that can be seen.

Make the geometry with code: math for curves and surfaces, loops and noise. Loop over surfaces, not over every cell of a bounding box: for each column or each angle, compute where the surface is and place the blocks there. In its 5 seconds a program can place about 3,000,000 blocks with mc.set, or test about 20 million cells with a little math each (fewer with heavier math such as sin and cos); a program that runs out of time places nothing. Build one part per program, but use no more programs than the build needs: every program that changes the world is followed by a full run of the whole world in Minecraft. Keep the whole build under the limit of 3,000,000 blocks with shells two or three blocks deep, not solid volumes (a solid cube 250 blocks across is more than five times the limit): a closed shell two blocks deep reaches the limit at about 500 blocks across for a box and about 700 for a sphere, and three blocks deep at about 400 and 550. Keep program source short: write large tables to /work with bash and load them with mc.readFile (at most 8 MiB per file). After each program, read the result's minecraft field: blocks that fall, such as sand, gravel and concrete powder, will have moved. After each large part, look at it with see_build from far enough to judge its proportions and close enough to judge its detail: take overviews with size: "small", and details with region or clip.
